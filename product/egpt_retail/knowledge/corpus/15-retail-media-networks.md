## Executive Summary

Retail media networks (RMNs) are the fastest-growing segment in the global advertising industry, with US retail media ad spend reaching approximately $54.5 billion in 2024 and projected to exceed $107 billion by 2027 (eMarketer, 2025). The defining feature of an RMN is the use of a retailer's first-party purchase data — transaction history, loyalty programme data, and browsing behaviour — to deliver and measure advertising that connects directly to product sales. Unlike traditional digital advertising, retail media closes the loop between ad exposure and purchase because the retailer controls both the media inventory and the point of sale. Amazon Advertising alone generated $56.2 billion in advertising revenue in 2024 (Amazon Annual Report, 2024), representing roughly 7.6% of all global digital ad spend and more than the entire combined television advertising budgets of the US's four major broadcast networks. For CPG brands, retail media has shifted from a supplemental activation tactic to the primary performance advertising channel, in many cases consuming 40–60% of total digital media budgets (GroupM, 2025).

**Why this matters**: Retailers that build scaled, data-rich media networks can generate advertising profit margins of 70–90% on media revenue (BCG, 2024) — structurally higher than the 1–3% net margins typical of grocery retail — fundamentally changing the economics of who earns the most profit in the consumer goods value chain.

## 1. What Is Retail Media

### 1.1 Definition and Structural Significance

**Retail Media Network (RMN)** *(also: commerce media network, retail advertising platform)*
- **Definition**: A formal advertising business operated by a retailer that packages its owned digital properties (website, app, email, connected TV), in-store physical media (digital screens, shelf-edge displays, audio), and its first-party shopper data into a structured, self-serve or managed advertising offering sold to brands and suppliers.
- **Mechanics**: An RMN monetises three distinct asset types simultaneously: (1) **owned inventory** — ad placements within the retailer's own digital and physical footprint; (2) **first-party data** — purchase and behavioural data used to target ads on external platforms; and (3) **measurement capability** — closed-loop attribution linking ad spend directly to in-store or online sales. Revenue flows from brand advertisers (primarily CPG manufacturers, pharmaceutical brands, and endemic suppliers) to the retailer in exchange for visibility, targeting, and verified sales lift. The retailer's media business is structurally separate from its core merchandising P&L, with dedicated ad technology teams, sales forces, and reporting infrastructure.
- **Example**: Walmart Connect generated $4.4 billion in advertising revenue in FY2024 (Walmart Annual Report, 2024), representing approximately 1.4% of Walmart's total US net sales. At an estimated 70% operating margin on media revenue (BCG estimate, 2024), Walmart Connect contributed roughly $3.1 billion in operating profit — more than the operating income generated by Walmart's entire Sam's Club division. This illustrates how media revenue, even at a fraction of total sales volume, can become a disproportionate profit driver.
- **Business Implication**: A VP of commercial strategy or category director at a CPG brand must treat retail media investment as a core trade investment decision — not a marketing budget line — because the largest retailers are increasingly tying shelf placement, promotional support, and new item listings to brands' participation and spend levels in their media programmes.

**Why Retail Media Is the Fastest-Growing Ad Channel**
- **Definition**: Retail media's growth rate of approximately 22% CAGR between 2020 and 2025 (eMarketer, 2025) stems from three structural tailwinds converging simultaneously.
- **Mechanics**: First, the deprecation of third-party cookies and mobile advertising IDs (Apple's ATT framework reduced iPhone ad targeting accuracy by an estimated 60%, per Snap earnings call, 2021) destroyed the targeting infrastructure that funded brand advertising on Google and Meta, pushing spend toward alternatives with verified first-party data. Second, the proof of Amazon's advertising model — high-intent search queries combined with purchase data — validated that retail environments generate conversion rates 4–10x higher than standard display advertising (Amazon Ads benchmark data, 2024). Third, the COVID-driven acceleration of e-commerce (US e-commerce penetration jumped from 14% to 22% in 12 months, per Census Bureau, 2021) expanded the digital inventory that retailers could monetise. These three forces together created the conditions for retail media to absorb incremental brand budgets that had nowhere else to perform with equivalent measurability.
- **Example**: After Apple's ATT rollout in Q2 2021, Meta reported a $10 billion annualised revenue impact. Simultaneously, Amazon Advertising revenue grew 32% in FY2021 to $31.2 billion, directly absorbing budgets from brands seeking verifiable purchase-linked outcomes (Amazon Annual Report, 2021).
- **Business Implication**: Retailers with even modest loyalty programme scale (1–2 million active households) now have a monetisable first-party data asset. Retailers should assess their data density — number of transactions per customer per year, SKU-level purchase history depth, cross-category breadth — before approaching brands about media partnerships, because data quality determines CPM premiums attainable versus open-web programmatic inventory.

### 1.2 Endemic and Non-Endemic Advertisers

**Endemic Advertiser**
- **Definition**: A brand or supplier that sells its products through the retailer's own stores or platform, purchasing advertising within that retailer's media network specifically to drive visibility and conversion for products it already has listed in that retailer's assortment.
- **Mechanics**: Endemic advertisers are the foundational revenue base of every RMN — they are the CPG manufacturers, private label competitors, and national brand suppliers who have existing vendor relationships with the retailer. Because they sell through the retailer, their advertising is directly measurable against sales on that retailer's platform: a Kellogg's sponsored product ad on Kroger.com drives Kellogg's cereal sales at Kroger, which is attributable at the SKU level. Endemic advertising spend is typically funded from trade promotion budgets — co-op advertising, shopper marketing, or account-specific digital activation funds — rather than national brand media budgets, making it a near-guaranteed revenue stream as retailers scale their self-serve platforms.
- **Example**: Procter & Gamble is Walmart Connect's single largest endemic advertiser, spending an estimated $1B+ annually across Walmart's on-site, in-store, and off-site media products for brands including Tide, Pampers, Gillette, and Oral-B (Walmart Investor Day, 2024). P&G's Walmart Connect investment is structured as an account-level joint business plan commitment — a guaranteed minimum annual media spend agreed at the start of each fiscal year in exchange for preferential shelf placement, new item listing speed, and dedicated account team support.
- **Business Implication**: Category managers should model endemic retail media spend as a percentage of category sales at each retailer — the industry benchmark for mature CPG brands is 0.5–1.5% of retailer-specific net sales allocated to that retailer's media network — and treat it as a cost of retaining share of search, not as discretionary marketing spend.

**Non-Endemic Advertiser** *(also: non-endemic brand, out-of-category advertiser)*
- **Definition**: A brand that does not sell products through the retailer but purchases advertising within the retailer's media network to reach the retailer's shopper audience for its own marketing objectives — typically from categories such as financial services, automotive, travel, insurance, entertainment, or telecommunications.
- **Mechanics**: Non-endemic advertising unlocks a structurally larger total addressable market for retailers: instead of selling only to the 500–3,000 CPG suppliers who manufacture products sold in their stores, the retailer can sell to any brand seeking their specific audience demographic. A grocery retailer with 30 million households purchasing weekly has a more valuable audience for a credit card company, auto insurance brand, or streaming service than most open-web publishers — and the retailer's purchase data enables household income inference, family composition, and brand affinity targeting that no generic demographic audience can match. Non-endemic advertisers typically purchase off-site programmatic placements (using retailer first-party data on external DSPs) or premium in-store and digital display placements where contextual adjacency is less critical.
- **Example**: Kroger Precision Marketing generates an estimated 25–30% of its total media revenue from non-endemic advertisers as of 2024 (Insider Intelligence, 2024). Chase Bank has been a recurring KPM non-endemic advertiser since 2021, purchasing Kroger shopper audience segments (high-spending households, frequent flyer purchasers, premium category buyers) for Chase Sapphire credit card acquisition campaigns — delivered via programmatic display and paid search, targeted at Kroger loyalty members who match Chase's ideal cardholder profile. KPM reports non-endemic campaigns in financial services deliver match rates of 65–75% between Kroger loyalty emails and major identity graphs, enabling precision targeting unavailable through first-party bank data alone (Kroger Precision Marketing, 2024).
- **Business Implication**: A retail media sales director should build a dedicated non-endemic sales team and rate card separate from the CPG/endemic sales team — because non-endemic advertisers have different buying cycles (annual upfront commitments, brand campaign rhythms), different measurement expectations (brand lift and audience reach rather than ROAS), and materially higher CPMs ($30–80 for non-endemic audience segments versus $8–25 for endemic sponsored products) that require different pitch materials and success metrics.

> **Key Takeaway**: Retail media is structurally different from traditional advertising because the seller of the advertising also sells the product being advertised — meaning attribution is direct, deterministic, and based on actual transactions rather than modelled proxies. Non-endemic advertising extends this model to any brand that values shopper audience data, making the retailer's total advertising TAM far larger than its supplier base alone.

## 2. On-Site Retail Media

### 2.1 Sponsored Products and Search Advertising

**Sponsored Products** *(also: product listing ads, keyword-targeted search ads)*
- **Definition**: Paid placements that surface a specific product listing in retailer search results or category browse pages, triggered by shopper search queries or category navigation, billed on a cost-per-click (CPC) basis.
- **Mechanics**: An advertiser selects target keywords (e.g., "organic peanut butter"), sets a maximum CPC bid, and the retailer's ad server auctions the sponsored placement in real time when a shopper performs a matching search. The winning ad appears in a premium position — typically the first 1–4 results above organic listings, and interspersed within the results grid. The auction winner is determined by a combination of bid amount and quality score (predicted click-through rate multiplied by relevance to the search query). Click-through rate (CTR) for sponsored products averages 0.5–1.5% on retailer search pages, versus 0.05–0.1% for standard display (Criteo Retail Media Benchmarks, 2024). Organic search ranking is earned through relevance algorithms (conversion rate, ratings, in-stock status); sponsored positions are purchased atop organic results.
- **Example**: On Amazon, sponsored products occupy an estimated 55–65% of the first page of search results for competitive CPG categories such as vitamins, coffee, and snack foods (Jungle Scout, 2024). Brands such as Kellogg's report that sponsored product placements generate approximately 35–45% of their total Amazon-attributable revenue, with ROAS (return on ad spend) of 4–8x depending on category competitiveness.
- **Business Implication**: A brand's Amazon search share of voice (SOV) — the percentage of sponsored impressions won for target keywords — directly correlates with organic search rank improvement over 6–12 months, because sponsored clicks generate conversion signals that the Amazon A9 algorithm uses to improve organic ranking. Brands that pause sponsored products to cut costs often see their organic rank fall within 8–12 weeks, turning what appears to be a cost savings into long-term revenue loss.

**Sponsored Brands** *(also: headline search ads)*
- **Definition**: A banner-format ad unit at the top of retailer search results that showcases a brand logo, custom headline, and three or more products simultaneously, linking to a brand-customised landing page or store within the retailer's platform.
- **Mechanics**: Sponsored brands operate on a CPC model and are designed for upper-funnel brand awareness combined with mid-funnel product consideration. Unlike sponsored products (single SKU), sponsored brands allow a multi-product showcase — enabling brands to cross-sell complementary items (e.g., pasta + sauce, shampoo + conditioner) in a single ad unit. Click-through rates average 0.3–0.7%, lower than sponsored products, but sponsored brand clicks generate 30–40% higher basket sizes due to multi-product discovery (Amazon Ads, 2024).
- **Example**: Procter & Gamble uses Amazon sponsored brands to run category domination strategies during peak seasons — booking sponsored brand headline placements for "laundry detergent" simultaneously with sponsored product placements for Tide, Gain, and Downy, ensuring P&G captures the top banner and the first four organic-looking rows. P&G reported that this "brand blocking" tactic reduced competitor click share on their core keywords by 18–24% during Q4 campaign periods (P&G Investor Presentation, 2023).
- **Business Implication**: Category managers should evaluate sponsored brands not just as awareness tools but as competitive defence mechanisms — controlling the visual real estate at the top of high-volume category search pages prevents competitors from intercepting shoppers at the highest-intent moment.

**Display Ads on Retailer Websites and Apps**
- **Definition**: Banner, carousel, and interstitial ad units served within a retailer's digital properties (homepage, category pages, product detail pages, cart page) that are targeted using shopper behavioural and purchase data.
- **Mechanics**: Retailer display is served by the retailer's proprietary ad server (or a third-party platform like CitrusAd or Criteo) and targeted using purchase history segments — e.g., "buyers of competitor products in the last 90 days" or "lapsed buyers of Brand X." CPMs for retailer-served display range from $15–60 (Criteo, 2024), compared to $2–5 for open-web programmatic, because purchase-intent targeting significantly increases click probability. Retailer display ads link directly to product pages, enabling same-session conversion measurement.
- **Example**: Target's Roundel (its retail media network) serves display ads on Target.com and the Target app, with CPMs in the $30–50 range for purchase-intent segments. Target reports that Roundel-served display ads achieve 3–5x higher click-through rates than standard programmatic display because they leverage purchase history (Target Roundel Media Kit, 2024). A brand advertising "diaper" display ads to Target loyalty members who purchased diapers in the last 60 days achieves a CTR of approximately 1.2% vs. the 0.07% industry average for standard display.
- **Business Implication**: A media buyer should prioritise retailer display inventory over open-web programmatic for brand categories where purchase history is the most predictive targeting signal — particularly commodity categories (pet food, household cleaning, baby care) where shopper brand loyalty is stable and retargeting recent buyers generates measurable reactivation.

> **Key Takeaway**: On-site retail media occupies the highest-intent real estate in digital advertising — the moment a shopper is actively searching for a product — making it structurally more efficient than any other digital ad format at driving immediate purchase.

## 3. Off-Site Retail Media

### 3.1 Retailer Data for External Platform Targeting

**Off-Site Retail Media** *(also: extended audience, data syndication, retail data partnerships)*
- **Definition**: The use of a retailer's first-party shopper data to deliver targeted advertising on external digital platforms — including programmatic display networks, social media, connected TV (CTV), and search — where the retailer does not own the media inventory but licenses its data to enable superior targeting.
- **Mechanics**: The retailer hashes and segments its loyalty programme data (email addresses, purchase history) and pushes those segments to demand-side platforms (DSPs) such as The Trade Desk, or directly to social platforms via customer list uploads (Meta Custom Audiences, Pinterest Actalike). Brands pay the retailer a data fee (CPM premium of $3–10 on top of underlying media cost) to access those audience segments. The critical advantage is match rate: a retailer with 50 million loyalty members whose email addresses match Facebook's identity graph at a 60–70% rate can deliver addressable advertising to 30–35 million verified purchasers on Facebook — a target precision that is structurally unavailable through Meta's own interest-based targeting.
- **Example**: Kroger Precision Marketing (KPM) licenses its 60-million-household purchase data asset to brands running programmatic advertising across the open web via The Trade Desk. A CPG brand running an off-site campaign through KPM pays approximately $4–7 CPM for the data fee, on top of the $2–4 CPM programmatic media cost — total effective CPM of $6–11 versus comparable open-web targeting at $3–5 CPM. KPM reports that off-site campaigns using Kroger purchase data deliver an average ROAS of 5.4x, compared to 2.1x for the same media placed without Kroger targeting (Kroger Precision Marketing Case Studies, 2024).
- **Business Implication**: A brand media planner should budget for off-site retail media as a distinct line item separate from both on-site retail media and open-web programmatic — because the marginal value of precise purchase-intent targeting justifies a 2–3x CPM premium when the campaign objective is converting competitor brand buyers or reactivating lapsed purchasers, outcomes that demographic and interest targeting cannot reliably deliver.

**Programmatic Off-Site Channels**

| Channel | Format | Data Application | ROAS Benchmark |
|---|---|---|---|
| Programmatic display (open web) | Banner, rich media | Purchase segment targeting via DSP | 2–4x |
| Paid social (Meta, Pinterest) | Image, video, carousel | Customer list matching + lookalike audiences | 3–6x |
| Connected TV (CTV) | 15/30-sec video | Household purchase targeting on Hulu, Peacock | 1.5–3x (brand lift focus) |
| Streaming audio (Spotify, Pandora) | 30-sec audio | Category buyer segments | 1.5–2.5x |
| Paid search (Google) | Text + shopping ads | Purchase history segment bidding | 4–8x |

> **Key Takeaway**: Off-site retail media allows brands to extend a retailer's purchase intelligence across the entire open internet, turning a 30-million-member loyalty database into a targeting asset that rivals Google and Meta in precision for in-category buyer identification.

## 4. In-Store Retail Media

### 4.1 Digital Screens and Physical Media Formats

**In-Store Retail Media** *(also: in-store digital media, physical retail media, point-of-purchase media)*
- **Definition**: Advertising placements within a physical store environment — including digital screens at shelf, endcaps, checkout, and entrance; audio announcements; shelf-edge displays; and smart cart screens — that reach shoppers at the moment of purchase decision.
- **Mechanics**: In-store media is the highest-intent advertising format that exists: the shopper is physically present in the store, product in category. A digital screen at the pasta aisle endcap advertising a pasta sauce promotion reaches a shopper who is already 3 feet from the product. Dwell time at key store zones (deli counter: 4–6 minutes; checkout queue: 3–5 minutes; pharmacy counter: 2–4 minutes) provides adequate exposure time for video and dynamic content. Attribution is achieved by matching in-store ad exposure (via loyalty card scan at checkout) to basket data — if a shopper with a known loyalty ID is exposed to an in-store screen ad and then purchases the advertised product, that conversion is attributed to the in-store placement.
- **Example**: Walmart operates over 170,000 digital screens across its ~4,600 US stores, managed through its Walmart Luminate in-store media platform. Walmart's screen network is the largest in-store digital media network in the US by screen count, reaching approximately 140 million unique weekly shoppers (Walmart Connect, 2024). CPMs for Walmart in-store display screens are approximately $8–15 — significantly lower than on-site digital ($15–30 CPM) due to attribution complexity, but the format is unique in that no digital channel can replicate its physical proximity to the point of purchase.
- **Business Implication**: A retail media director should price in-store media at a premium over programmatic display by quantifying its exclusive attribute — physical proximity — rather than competing on CPM efficiency metrics, because the value of reaching a shopper 3 feet from a shelf cannot be replicated by any digital ad unit, regardless of targeting precision.

**Audio Retail Media**
- **Definition**: Branded audio announcements, sponsored content segments, or musical playlists delivered through a store's overhead PA system, triggered by time-of-day, category, or promotional schedule.
- **Mechanics**: Platforms such as VIBENOMICS (acquired by Mood Media, 2022) enable CPG brands to schedule audio ads within the in-store PA rotation. A brand pays per play or per store per week; the ad runs during high-traffic windows. Attribution is softer than screen-based media — no impression-level data at the individual customer level — but aggregate lift studies using control/test store cohorts can measure sales impact across a campaign period. In-store audio reaches 100% of shoppers in the store, versus digital screens that require physical proximity to a display.
- **Example**: Walgreens deployed VIBENOMICS audio advertising across 5,000+ stores in 2023, allowing pharmaceutical and FMCG brands to purchase 30-second audio spots targeted by store geography and time of day. Walgreens reported that in-store audio campaigns for OTC health products generated an average 12–18% sales lift in stores where audio ads ran versus comparable stores without audio, measured via matched-store control methodology (Walgreens Advertising Group, 2023).
- **Business Implication**: Audio retail media is most effective for trial-generating promotions ("receive a $1 coupon at the pharmacy counter when you mention this offer") and event-driven activations (flu season, allergy season) rather than brand awareness, because its anonymous reach is its limitation while its in-store presence advantage is maximised by offers that require immediate action.

**Smart Carts and Shelf-Edge Displays**

**Smart Cart Media** *(also: connected cart advertising)*
- **Definition**: Digital screens embedded in shopping cart handles that display personalised ads, recipes, and promotions as shoppers navigate the store, triggered by location data, loyalty profile, and purchase history.
- **Mechanics**: Smart cart platforms (Instacart's Caper Cart, Veeve Smart Cart) include a digital screen, item scanning capability, and weight-based purchase verification. As the cart moves through the store, location triggers serve category-relevant ads — advertising coffee brands when the cart enters the coffee aisle. Advertisers pay CPM rates of $25–50 for smart cart placements because of the deterministic link between ad exposure, cart location, and purchase — the item is confirmed into the cart in the same session as the ad impression.
- **Example**: Stop & Shop (Ahold Delhaize) deployed 500 Instacart Caper Carts across 10 stores in 2023. Early results showed a 23% increase in basket size for smart cart users versus standard cart shoppers, attributed partly to on-screen suggested complements and recipe-driven add-ons. CPG brands advertising on Caper Carts in Stop & Shop stores reported an average click-to-add-to-cart rate of 8.2% — substantially higher than any digital ad format (Instacart Caper Cart Pilot Data, 2023).
- **Business Implication**: Smart cart advertising commands premium pricing because of its closed-loop, in-session measurability — ad impression, product scan, and transaction are all captured by the same device, creating the cleanest attribution data available in physical retail.

**Shelf-Edge Display** *(also: electronic shelf label advertising, ESL media, shelf-edge screen)*
- **Definition**: A digital or electronic display unit mounted at the product shelf level — at the price tag rail or aisle edge — that delivers dynamic branded content, promotional messaging, or video advertising to shoppers at the exact moment of product selection.
- **Mechanics**: Shelf-edge displays are networked across the store and managed through a central content scheduling system. Ad content is planned by brand, store zone, time-of-day, or promotional calendar — a CPG brand books a shelf-edge placement adjacent to its product's shelf location for a defined campaign period. When activated, the screen displays brand creative (price-and-promotion, product benefit messaging, or recipe content) within the shopper's direct line of sight as they evaluate competing products on the shelf. Attribution follows the same loyalty-card-to-transaction logic as other in-store media: shoppers in the exposed store zone who purchase the advertised product within the campaign period are attributed to the placement via basket data matching. Dwell time in the in-aisle product zone averages 25–40 seconds per visit (Shopper Marketing Institute, 2024), providing sufficient exposure for a static or short-loop video message.
- **Example**: Best Buy operates shelf-edge digital display screens at product shelf locations across its ~1,000 US stores as part of its Best Buy Ads retail media network, enabling brands such as Samsung, LG, and Bose to display video creative at the exact shelf location of their products. Best Buy reports that shelf-edge brand campaigns drive 20–35% higher consideration lift versus standard endcap displays because the creative runs at the exact moment a shopper is physically comparing competing products side by side (Best Buy Ads Partner Data, 2024). Kroger has separately piloted shelf-edge ESL advertising in select stores, enabling CPG brands to activate dynamic price-and-promotional content at shelf that updates in real time during promotional events without physical label changes.
- **Business Implication**: A category manager evaluating in-store media investment should prioritise shelf-edge placements for categories with long in-aisle dwell times and high on-the-spot decision rates — consumer electronics, wine, cosmetics, and specialty food — because the format's value compounds when the purchase decision is not yet made when the shopper reaches the shelf, making the advertising intervention directly causal to brand selection rather than simply reinforcing a prior brand preference formed at home.

> **Key Takeaway**: In-store retail media is the only advertising channel with simultaneous physical proximity to product and verified shopper identity — its CPM must be evaluated against its unique proximity premium, not against open-web digital benchmarks.

### 5.2 Amazon Advertising — Deep Profile

**Amazon Ads**
- **Definition**: Amazon's end-to-end advertising platform that spans keyword-triggered sponsored ads, programmatic display via the Amazon DSP, streaming video on Prime Video, and audio on Amazon Music — all connected to Amazon's purchase, search, and streaming data.
- **Mechanics**: Amazon Advertising operates on two tiers: (1) **self-serve sponsored ads** (Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display) purchased through the Amazon Ads Console by brands and agencies, with real-time auction-based pricing; and (2) the **Amazon DSP** (demand-side platform), a managed and self-serve programmatic buying platform that extends Amazon's audience data across Amazon-owned properties (IMDb, Twitch, Prime Video) and third-party sites. Attribution uses Amazon's closed-loop data: ad exposure is matched to Amazon purchases at the individual shopper level, producing verified ROAS that no external measurement vendor can replicate with equivalent accuracy.
- **Example**: Amazon's advertising revenue grew from $31.2 billion in FY2021 to $56.2 billion in FY2024 — an 80% increase in three years — driven by the expansion of Sponsored Display into new categories and the rapid growth of Prime Video advertising following the introduction of ads in January 2024. Prime Video advertising CPMs of $25–40 are significantly above streaming industry averages of $15–25, reflecting the premium advertisers pay for Amazon's purchase-intent targeting applied to streaming audiences (Amazon Annual Report, 2024).
- **Business Implication**: A brand selling on Amazon must view Amazon Advertising as a mandatory commercial investment rather than optional media, because the Amazon A9/A10 algorithm explicitly factors sponsored ad conversion history into organic search ranking — meaning brands that do not advertise on Amazon rank lower organically, creating a compounding disadvantage that is structurally impossible to overcome through organic content optimisation alone.

### 5.3 Walmart Connect — Deep Profile

**Walmart Connect**
- **Definition**: Walmart's retail media business offering on-site sponsored placements on Walmart.com, in-store digital screens across ~4,600 stores, social media advertising using Walmart's first-party data, and a programmatic DSP (powered by The Trade Desk partnership) for off-site audience extension.
- **Mechanics**: Walmart Connect operates on a hybrid model: a self-serve auction-based interface for sponsored products and display, and a managed service team for larger spend tiers (typically brands spending >$1M annually). Walmart's unique advantage versus Amazon is physical footprint: Walmart's 140 million weekly US store visitors — disproportionately from lower-income households and rural geographies — provide a targeting dataset that no digital-only platform can replicate. Walmart Connect's integration with The Trade Desk enables programmatic buyers to activate Walmart purchase segments across the entire open internet via a standardised programmatic interface.
- **Example**: Unilever invested an estimated $400 million+ in Walmart Connect annually as of 2023, using both on-site sponsored placements and off-site programmatic to reach Walmart loyalty shoppers. Unilever reported that Walmart Connect campaigns for personal care products (Dove, TRESemmé) delivered an average ROAS of 6.2x on-site and 4.1x off-site — significantly above the brand's overall digital advertising ROAS of 2.8x (Unilever Investor Day, 2023).
- **Business Implication**: For CPG brands with significant Walmart volume, Walmart Connect is as strategically critical as Walmart's traditional vendor relationship — brands that under-invest in Walmart media risk losing share of search and in-store visibility to competitors who do invest, especially as Walmart increasingly uses media investment as a signal of brand partnership commitment.

### 5.4 Instacart Ads — Deep Profile

**Instacart Ads**
- **Definition**: Instacart's retail media platform offering sponsored product placements, display advertising, and shoppable video formats within Instacart's grocery delivery app and website — powered by basket-composition and active-purchase-intent data from 8M+ monthly active shoppers across 1,400+ retail banner partners.
- **Mechanics**: Instacart Ads operates a self-serve auction platform (Instacart Ads Manager) for sponsored products with CPC pricing, and a managed service tier for display and video. What makes Instacart structurally distinct from other RMNs is the intent context: every shopper session on Instacart terminates in a grocery basket transaction — there is no browsing-only behaviour. Instacart's data advantage is basket-level context in real time: knowing that a shopper has already added Brand A's pasta to their active cart enables the platform to serve a complementary sauce ad or a competing brand's conquesting ad at that precise moment. Attribution is deterministic within the session: ad impression → add-to-cart → order completion, all captured within the same app interaction.
- **Example**: During Super Bowl week 2024, Frito-Lay purchased top-of-search placements on Instacart for the keywords "chips," "dips," and "snack mix," combined with display ads targeting Instacart shoppers who had purchased game-day snack categories in prior years. Frito-Lay reported a 4.2x ROAS on Instacart Super Bowl campaigns versus a 2.8x ROAS on comparable keyword targeting on Amazon — the higher efficiency attributable to Instacart's active-basket context versus Amazon's more diffuse purchase intent mix (Instacart Advertising Partner Update, 2024). Average CPCs on Instacart during Super Bowl week reached $3.80–4.20 for snack and beverage keywords — 2.5x normal CPCs — reflecting competitive demand from the top 20 snack and beverage brands simultaneously targeting the same high-intent audience.
- **Business Implication**: Food and beverage brands whose categories have high impulse-basket-building dynamics — snacks, beverages, prepared foods, condiments, fresh produce — should allocate a dedicated Instacart media budget separate from Amazon and Walmart Connect, because Instacart's active-grocery-session context generates add-to-cart conversion rates that on-site advertising at general merchandisers cannot match; underfunding Instacart allows competitors to intercept shoppers at the exact moment they are assembling their weekly grocery order.

### 5.5 Albertsons Media Collective — Profile

**Albertsons Media Collective (AMC)**

## 6. Tech Stack — Ad Serving and Platform Architecture

### 6.1 Ad Serving Platforms

**Retail Media Platform (RMP)** *(also: retail ad server, commerce media platform)*
- **Definition**: The technology infrastructure that powers a retailer's advertising business — including inventory management, auction-based bidding, ad serving, reporting, and billing — operating either as a proprietary build or a licensed SaaS solution from third-party vendors.
- **Mechanics**: An RMP connects three systems: (1) the retailer's **product catalogue and inventory feed** (ensuring ads only run for in-stock products); (2) the retailer's **identity and data layer** (matching shopper identity to purchase history for targeting and attribution); and (3) the **ad auction engine** (ranking competing bids using a second-price or modified first-price auction, considering both bid price and relevance score). The platform then serves the winning ad within the retailer's website or app and records the impression, click, and subsequent purchase against the advertiser's campaign.
- **Example**: CitrusAd (acquired by Publicis Groupe, 2021) powers the retail media programmes for Albertsons, Carrefour, and Shipt, enabling retailers to serve sponsored product placements through a centralised campaign management system. CitrusAd's shared multi-retailer infrastructure reduces the investment barrier for mid-market retailers who lack the internal resources to build a proprietary ad server. A retailer deploying CitrusAd can launch a sponsored products programme in 8–12 weeks versus 18–24 months for a proprietary build — a speed advantage that directly accelerates time-to-media-revenue (CitrusAd Platform Documentation, 2024).
- **Business Implication**: A retailer's choice of RMP vendor is a long-term strategic commitment — switching costs are high once brand advertiser campaigns, reporting workflows, and audience segments are built on a platform. Retailers should evaluate vendors on three criteria before signing: (1) revenue share percentage (15–25% for licensed platforms versus zero for proprietary builds); (2) self-serve capability — whether brands can access and manage campaigns independently without retailer involvement; and (3) off-site activation support, since retailers that cannot extend their audience data to external DSPs leave the fastest-growing segment of retail media revenue inaccessible.

**Criteo Commerce Media Platform**
- **Definition**: Criteo's end-to-end retail media technology suite covering on-site sponsored listings, off-site programmatic, and measurement — operating as both a technology licensor (powering retailer-owned RMNs) and a direct media network (Criteo Commerce Grid, connecting 700+ retailer inventory partners to brand advertisers).
- **Mechanics**: Criteo's Retail Media platform uses a unified identity layer (Criteo ID) to match shopper behaviour across 700+ retailer websites, enabling advertisers to purchase audience segments that span multiple retailers simultaneously — a capability no single retailer's proprietary platform can offer. Criteo processes approximately 100 billion shopping events per month, generating purchase-intent signals that feed its targeting and bidding algorithms (Criteo Annual Report, 2024).
- **Example**: Ulta Beauty deployed Criteo's Retail Media platform in 2022 to launch its UB Media retail media network, enabling 200+ beauty brands to purchase sponsored product placements on Ulta.com. Within 12 months, Ulta reported $200M+ in media revenue run rate, with a 4–6x average ROAS for sponsored products and a 3.5x ROAS for Criteo-powered off-site programmatic campaigns targeting Ulta purchase segments (Criteo Partner Case Study, 2023).
- **Business Implication**: A retailer evaluating Criteo as its RMP should weigh the multi-retailer network effect against the risk of audience commoditisation — because Criteo's Criteo ID matches shopper behaviour across 700+ retailer sites simultaneously, competing brand advertisers can purchase audience segments that include that retailer's shoppers without buying inventory exclusively through that retailer, diluting the data moat that justifies premium CPMs. Retailers with highly differentiated shopper demographics (Ulta's beauty-specialist audience, for example) benefit most from Criteo's cross-retailer reach, while retailers with commodity audiences may prefer a proprietary platform that keeps their first-party data exclusively within their own ad inventory.

**PromoteIQ (Microsoft)** *(also: Microsoft Retail Media)*

- **Mechanics**: PromoteIQ connects brand advertisers to retailer on-site inventory through a managed-service model, with Microsoft's AI-powered bidding optimisation and brand safety tools. The platform's integration with Microsoft's LinkedIn and Bing advertising data enables off-site activation of retail audiences across Microsoft's media properties — a capability unique among RMP vendors that enables targeting by professional role, company size, and B2B identity attributes alongside retail purchase history.
- **Example**: The Home Depot's retail media programme uses PromoteIQ infrastructure to serve sponsored product ads for 5,000+ brand suppliers across HomeDepot.com. The Home Depot reports that its retail media network (One Retail Media) generates significant incremental revenue, with brands such as Behr Paint, DeWalt, and Milwaukee Tools among its largest advertisers (The Home Depot Investor Day, 2024).

## 7. Revenue Models

### 7.1 Pricing Structures

**Cost Per Click (CPC)** *(also: pay-per-click, PPC)*
- **Definition**: An auction-based pricing model in which an advertiser pays a fixed or bid-determined fee each time a shopper clicks on a sponsored ad unit, regardless of how many times the ad is displayed.
- **Mechanics**: In CPC auctions, each advertiser sets a maximum bid (max CPC). The ad server ranks competing bids by a combination of bid amount and quality score (predicted CTR × relevance). The winner pays the second-highest bid plus $0.01 (second-price auction) or a modified first-price amount. Average CPCs on Amazon Sponsored Products range from $0.40 (long-tail keywords in niche categories) to $5.00+ (highly competitive keywords such as "protein powder" or "running shoes"). CPC's advantage for advertisers is that budget is only consumed when a shopper actively engages — not for passive impressions.
- **Example**: On Instacart, average CPCs for sponsored products in the grocery category range from $0.30–$1.50, with premium placement CPCs (top-of-page, category hero) reaching $2.50–$4.00 during high-demand periods such as Super Bowl week or Thanksgiving (Instacart Ads benchmarks, 2024). Brands managing Instacart campaigns with a target ROAS of 4x should set max CPC bids at approximately 25% of their product's gross margin per unit to maintain profitability at average conversion rates.
- **Business Implication**: CPC bidding requires continuous optimisation — a brand that sets bids and leaves them unmanaged for 30+ days typically overspends by 15–25% as competitive bids shift and keyword CPCs inflate, making weekly bid management non-negotiable for performance-focused retail media campaigns.

**Cost Per Mille (CPM)** *(also: cost per thousand impressions)*
- **Definition**: A pricing model in which an advertiser pays a fixed rate per 1,000 ad impressions delivered, used primarily for display, video, and in-store screen advertising where brand awareness and reach are the campaign objective.
- **Mechanics**: CPM pricing is used for ad formats where click-through is not the primary success metric — banner ads on retailer homepages, in-store screen placements, and connected TV spots all price on CPM. Retailer display CPMs range from $8 (in-store screens) to $60 (homepage takeovers during peak shopping events). The CPM model shifts impression delivery risk to the advertiser — the retailer guarantees impressions delivered, not outcomes achieved.
- **Example**: Target Roundel homepage takeover placements during Black Friday week are priced at CPMs of $40–65 (Roundel Media Kit, 2024). For a brand purchasing 10 million impressions at a $50 CPM, the total cost is $500,000. If the campaign generates a 0.5% CTR (50,000 clicks) and a 15% conversion rate from click to purchase (7,500 units sold), and each purchase averages $30, the ROAS is ($225,000 revenue / $500,000 spend) = 0.45x — meaning CPM display at these prices is not ROAS-efficient but is justified for brand awareness, competitive conquesting, or new product launch when reach matters more than immediate conversion.
- **Business Implication**: Media planners should not evaluate CPM-priced retail media inventory using ROAS benchmarks derived from CPC sponsored products — the two formats serve different funnel stages, and applying conversion ROAS to upper-funnel CPM inventory causes brands to incorrectly under-invest in awareness-stage retail media that drives organic search volume and long-term market share.

**Flat Fee / Sponsorships**
- **Definition**: A negotiated fixed-cost arrangement for a defined placement or time period — such as a retailer's weekly circular feature, an email spotlight, a checkout page banner, or an exclusive category share-of-voice buyout — with no variable cost based on clicks or impressions delivered.
- **Mechanics**: Flat fee arrangements are common for high-visibility placements where inventory is scarce (retailer homepage, app launch screen, endcap digital displays) and where the placement's value is intrinsic to its prominence rather than its measurable performance. A brand pays a fixed fee for exclusivity — e.g., being the only advertiser in the category featured in the retailer's weekly email newsletter.
- **Example**: Kroger's weekly digital circular sponsorship placements for premium food categories cost $150,000–$300,000 per week for a featured brand position, with guaranteed reach to Kroger's 30M+ digital circular recipients (Kroger Media Rate Card, 2024). For a brand launching a new product, a 4-week circular sponsorship at $200,000/week represents $800,000 but delivers reach to 120M+ total impressions across 4 weeks — an effective CPM of $6.67, competitive with broad-reach digital formats.
- **Business Implication**: Category managers should include flat fee retail media sponsorships in new product launch budgets specifically because they guarantee reach milestones irrespective of auction dynamics — unlike CPC placements where a new product with no conversion history will receive poor auction scores and under-deliver impressions during the critical launch window.

**Cost Per Acquisition (CPA)** *(also: performance-based media)*
- **Definition**: A pricing model in which an advertiser pays only when a specific conversion action is completed — typically a product purchase — making the retailer assume impression and click delivery risk in exchange for a higher per-conversion fee.
- **Mechanics**: CPA arrangements are uncommon in retail media because they require the retailer to bear media delivery risk, which conflicts with the economics of ad network operation. However, some retailers offer CPA-adjacent models through affiliate or performance programmes — particularly for new customer acquisition where brands value first-time buyers more than repeat purchasers. The CPA fee is typically set as a percentage of transaction value (5–20%) or a fixed fee per new customer acquired.
- **Example**: Instacart's Performance Plus programme offers brands a CPA-adjacent bidding option in which the Instacart algorithm automatically optimises bids to a target cost-per-purchase, shifting the optimisation burden from the brand to Instacart's algorithm. Brands using Performance Plus report 15–25% improvement in cost efficiency versus manual CPC management, at the cost of reduced transparency into individual keyword-level bid logic (Instacart Advertising, 2024).
- **Business Implication**: A trade marketing manager should reserve CPA-structured retail media arrangements specifically for new product launches and new customer acquisition programmes — where the brand's willingness to pay is tied to a verified first-time buyer — rather than applying CPA pricing to mature products with high organic repeat-purchase rates, where CPA fees on orders that would have occurred without advertising represent pure margin erosion with no incremental acquisition value.

> **Key Takeaway**: Retail media pricing models must be matched to campaign objective — CPC for performance/conversion, CPM for awareness and reach, flat fee for guaranteed visibility at launch, and CPA for new customer acquisition programmes. Applying a single pricing model across all retail media objectives is the most common and costly planning error.

## 8. Closed-Loop Measurement and Attribution

### 8.1 Closed-Loop Attribution

**Closed-Loop Measurement** *(also: purchase-based attribution, sales-linked measurement)*
- **Definition**: An attribution methodology that connects a specific ad exposure event (impression or click) to a verified product purchase using the retailer's own transaction data, without relying on third-party cookies, survey panels, or modelled proxy metrics.
- **Mechanics**: The retailer's ad server records a timestamped impression or click event against an anonymised shopper ID (derived from loyalty card, app login, or order ID). When the same shopper subsequently purchases the advertised product within an attribution window (typically 7–14 days post-click, 1–7 days post-purchase for display), the transaction is attributed to the campaign. The retailer controls all three data assets simultaneously: the identity (who clicked), the ad event (what they were shown), and the transaction (what they bought). This deterministic linkage is structurally impossible for any external ad platform to replicate, because Google and Meta have the click data but not the purchase transaction data for offline retail.
- **Example**: Amazon's closed-loop attribution reports exact units sold and revenue generated within 7 days of a sponsored product click, directly within the Amazon Ads console. This means a brand advertising Tide Pods on Amazon knows precisely: 12,500 clicks generated 1,875 purchases at an average order value of $18.50 = $34,687 in attributed revenue on $8,200 in ad spend = ROAS of 4.23x. No external measurement vendor can provide this precision for Amazon sales, because Amazon controls both the click and the transaction record (Amazon Ads Attribution, 2024).
- **Business Implication**: A media measurement analyst should distinguish between retailer-reported closed-loop attribution (deterministic, purchase-verified) and third-party multi-touch attribution (probabilistic, modelled) when presenting retail media ROAS to leadership — the two are not comparable, and conflating them overstates the performance of retailer-owned media and understates the uncertainty in non-retailer channel attribution.

### 8.2 Incrementality Testing

**Incrementality Testing** *(also: lift measurement, treatment-control testing, A/B sales lift study)*
- **Definition**: A controlled experiment methodology that measures the additional sales generated by an advertising campaign above what would have occurred organically without the campaign — isolating the causal effect of advertising from confounding factors such as organic demand, seasonality, and brand equity.
- **Mechanics**: The retailer (or a third-party measurement vendor) randomly assigns shoppers in the loyalty database to a **treatment group** (exposed to ads) and a **holdout control group** (not served ads). Both groups are matched on baseline purchase behaviour (same category purchase frequency, basket size, geography). After the campaign period, the sales difference between treatment and control is calculated as the **incremental ROAS (iROAS)**, which is structurally more conservative than raw ROAS because it excludes purchases that would have occurred without advertising. Industry studies consistently show that iROAS is 40–65% lower than reported ROAS (GroupM Incrementality Research, 2024), meaning a campaign reporting 6x ROAS may have an iROAS of only 2.5x.
- **Example**: Kroger Precision Marketing offers brands standardised incrementality testing as a paid measurement service (approximately $50,000–$150,000 per study depending on campaign scale). A KPM incrementality study for a major CPG brand in the laundry category found that sponsored search campaigns generated a 3.8x raw ROAS but only a 1.9x iROAS — meaning 50% of attributed sales would have occurred without advertising. The brand used this finding to shift 30% of its sponsored search budget to off-site programmatic, where incremental reach of non-buying consumers was higher (KPM Measurement Case Study, 2024).
- **Business Implication**: Brands that rely exclusively on raw ROAS for retail media optimisation systematically over-invest in campaigns that capture already-committed buyers (high raw ROAS, low iROAS) and under-invest in campaigns targeting non-buyers or competitor brand buyers (lower raw ROAS but higher iROAS). An iROAS-first measurement framework reallocates budget toward genuine new demand creation rather than credit-claiming for organic purchases.

**Attribution Window Standards**

| Attribution Window | Common Use | Typical Platform | ROAS Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-day click | Impulse categories (snack, beverage) | Amazon, Instacart | Highest ROAS (tightest window) |
| 7-day click | Grocery, FMCG standard | Amazon, Walmart, Kroger | Standard benchmark |
| 14-day click | Planned purchase categories (health, home) | Target, Walgreens | Lower ROAS (longer consideration) |
| 1-day view-through | Brand awareness display | Programmatic DSPs | Very low ROAS; used for awareness reporting |
| 30-day post-impression | CTV, upper funnel | Amazon DSP, Roku | Lowest; used only for brand lift |

> **Key Takeaway**: The attribution window chosen determines the ROAS reported — a 1-day attribution window can report 2–3x higher ROAS than a 14-day window for the same campaign, making cross-network ROAS comparisons meaningless unless windows are standardised.

## 9. Retail Media for Brands and Advertisers

### 9.1 Planning and Buying Retail Media Campaigns

**Retail Media Planning Framework**
- **Definition**: The structured process by which a CPG or brand advertiser allocates retail media budget across retailers, formats, and campaign objectives — integrating trade marketing investment logic with digital media buying methodology.
- **Mechanics**: Retail media planning requires integration of three traditionally separate budget streams: (1) **trade promotion spend** (historically managed by shopper marketing teams at the retailer account level); (2) **digital media spend** (managed by marketing/media agencies using reach and frequency metrics); and (3) **brand performance marketing** (managed by e-commerce teams using ROAS). As these three streams converge into retail media, brands face an organisational challenge — the function that controls retail media budget (trade vs. marketing) varies by company, creating inefficiencies when trade-funded and marketing-funded campaigns run simultaneously for the same product at the same retailer without coordination.

- **Business Implication**: Any CPG brand spending more than $20M annually on retail media should audit whether trade marketing and digital marketing teams are independently purchasing retail media at the same retailers — self-competition in ad auctions inflates CPCs and suppresses ROAS for both teams simultaneously.

## 10. Retail Media Economics

### 10.1 Why Retail Media Generates Disproportionate Profit

**Retail Media Operating Economics**
- **Definition**: The financial structure of a retail media network, characterised by very high operating margins (60–90%) relative to core retail operations (1–5% in grocery), creating a business-within-a-business that can generate more total operating profit per dollar of media revenue than any product sold in the retailer's stores.
- **Mechanics**: Retail media's high margins stem from structural characteristics: (1) **Zero inventory cost** — a sponsored product placement costs the retailer nothing in procurement, carrying cost, or shrinkage; (2) **Incremental revenue on existing infrastructure** — the retailer's website already exists; each additional ad impression served has near-zero marginal cost; (3) **Inelastic advertiser demand** — CPG brands have few alternatives for in-aisle, point-of-purchase advertising, making them relatively price-insensitive to CPM/CPC increases; (4) **First-party data moat** — competitors cannot easily replicate 60 million loyalty households' transaction history, creating pricing power. The primary cost of running an RMN is technology infrastructure, a sales force, and data science capabilities — fixed costs that become increasingly profitable as media revenue scales.
- **Example**: Amazon's advertising segment reported an operating margin of approximately 45–55% in FY2024 (estimated from segment disclosures), while Amazon's North America retail segment operates at approximately 6–7% operating margin. This means Amazon's advertising division generates roughly 8x more operating profit per revenue dollar than its core retail business. Amazon would be among the top-10 most profitable advertising companies in the world if it were a standalone entity (Amazon Annual Report, 2024; eMarketer analysis, 2024).
- **Business Implication**: For a grocery retailer operating at a 2% net margin, adding $500M in annual media revenue at a 70% margin generates $350M in operating profit — equivalent to the operating profit from approximately $17.5 billion in additional grocery sales. This asymmetry means that every incremental dollar of media revenue is structurally worth 35x more in profit terms than an incremental dollar of grocery sales, explaining why every major retailer is now investing aggressively to scale their RMN.

## 11. Data Clean Rooms

### 11.1 Privacy-Safe Data Collaboration

**Data Clean Room** *(also: privacy-preserving collaboration environment, secure data collaboration)*
- **Definition**: A secure, controlled computational environment in which two or more parties — typically a retailer and a brand advertiser — can run joint analyses on their combined datasets without either party having access to the other's raw, individually identifiable data.
- **Mechanics**: In a retail media data clean room, the retailer brings its transaction and loyalty data; the brand brings its CRM, media exposure, and first-party data. The clean room applies privacy controls — minimum group size rules, statistical noise injection, and result aggregation floors — that prevent either party from reverse-engineering individual-level records from the outputs. Queries run within the clean room return aggregated results (e.g., "Of the 500,000 households that saw Brand X's TV ad in Q3, what was their in-store purchase rate at Kroger in the 30 days following ad exposure?"). This answers campaign measurement questions that neither party could answer independently — Kroger doesn't have the TV ad exposure data; the brand doesn't have the purchase transaction data.
- **Example**: Kroger and Google deployed a joint data clean room integration in 2023 that enabled CPG brands to match Google campaign exposure (YouTube, Display, Search) to Kroger in-store and online purchase outcomes. Brands using the Kroger-Google clean room reported that they could for the first time attribute Google campaign spend to exact product unit sales at Kroger, with statistical confidence levels previously unavailable. Early results from 15 brand pilots showed an average 22% improvement in campaign ROI from optimisations enabled by clean room insights (Google/Kroger Clean Room Announcement, 2023).
- **Business Implication**: A data analytics director at a CPG brand should prioritise establishing data clean room agreements with their top 3–5 retail partners, because clean rooms are the only privacy-compliant mechanism to answer the fundamental question of whether the brand's non-retail advertising (TV, digital, out-of-home) is driving in-store sales — information that is commercially unavailable through any other means following the deprecation of third-party tracking.

## 12. The Future of Retail Media

### 12.1 Standardisation Challenges

**Retail Media Standardisation**
- **Definition**: The industry effort led by the IAB and MRC to establish common definitions, measurement methodologies, reporting taxonomies, and attribution standards across all retail media networks, enabling brands to compare performance across networks using consistent metrics.
- **Mechanics**: As of 2025, each major RMN reports metrics using different methodologies — Amazon uses a 7-day attribution window; Walmart uses a 14-day window; Instacart uses a 7-day window; Kroger KPM uses a 14-day window. A brand running the same campaign at Amazon and Kroger simultaneously cannot directly compare ROAS across the two networks because the attribution windows differ. The IAB's Retail Media Standards initiative (launched 2023) is developing a unified taxonomy covering: impression definitions, attribution window standards, ROAS calculation methodology, audience segment naming conventions, and reporting interface schemas. IAB published version 1.0 of the Retail Media Standards in Q2 2024, with adoption targeted by 2026 for major RMNs.
- **Example**: The IAB Retail Media Standards Working Group includes Amazon, Walmart, Target, Kroger, Instacart, and 30+ CPG brand members including P&G, Unilever, and Nestlé. The working group's 2024 report estimated that standardisation would save CPG brands an average of $2.3M annually in measurement reconciliation costs — time currently spent by data analysts manually normalising cross-network reporting to account for differing attribution windows and impression counting methodologies (IAB Retail Media Standards, 2024).
- **Business Implication**: Until standardisation is achieved, brand media teams should maintain a single internal methodology for cross-network comparison — normalising all RMN reporting to a common attribution window (typically 7-day last-click) before comparing ROAS across networks, to avoid incorrect budget allocation decisions driven by methodology differences rather than genuine performance differences.

### 12.2 Connected TV and Streaming Integration

**Retail Media on Connected TV (CTV)**
- **Definition**: The delivery of brand advertising within streaming TV content (Hulu, Peacock, Roku, Prime Video) targeted using retailer first-party purchase data, enabling household-level targeting precision previously unavailable in linear television advertising.
- **Mechanics**: Amazon's Prime Video introduced advertising in January 2024, immediately creating the largest CTV inventory combined with the largest retail purchase dataset in the world. Brands can purchase Prime Video ad inventory and target it using Amazon purchase segments — e.g., "households that have purchased pet food at least 6 times in the last 12 months" — with attribution measured as subsequent Amazon or physical store purchases. CPMs for retail-targeted CTV range from $25–40 versus $10–20 for non-targeted streaming inventory, with incremental ROAS of 1.5–3.0x (Amazon DSP data, 2024).
- **Example**: Chewy launched a CTV campaign on Amazon Prime Video in Q1 2024 targeting Amazon pet supply buyers who had not purchased from Chewy in the previous 12 months. By using Amazon purchase data to identify the target household set, then serving 30-second Chewy video ads during Prime Video content, Chewy reported a 14% new customer conversion rate from exposed households versus a 2.3% baseline rate for non-targeted CTV — a 6x lift attributable to the precision of retail purchase targeting applied to streaming video inventory (Amazon DSP Case Study, 2024).
- **Business Implication**: For CPG categories with high household penetration (pet, baby, household care), CTV targeted with retail purchase data represents the only scalable video advertising format with purchase-intent targeting at meaningful reach levels — brands should shift 15–25% of linear TV budgets to retail-data-targeted CTV by 2026 as Prime Video, Peacock (Walmart data partnership), and Hulu (Target data partnership) expand retail-addressable inventory.

### 12.3 Future Trends — AI, Measurement Unification, and Non-Endemic Growth

**AI-Powered Dynamic Pricing for Retail Media Inventory**
- **Definition**: The use of machine learning algorithms to dynamically price ad inventory in real time based on predicted conversion probability, competitive auction pressure, and campaign performance signals — moving retail media pricing beyond static CPM/CPC rate cards toward fully dynamic auction pricing.
- **Mechanics**: First-generation retail media platforms use standard second-price auctions with quality score multipliers. Next-generation platforms use predictive models trained on billions of purchase events to forecast the probability that a given shopper, at a given moment, in a given product context, will purchase an advertised item — and price the impression accordingly. This shifts bid optimisation from human bid managers making weekly adjustments to real-time algorithmic execution at impression level.
- **Example**: Amazon's Dynamic Bidding feature (launched 2019, expanded 2023) automatically adjusts a brand's CPC bids up to 100% above the set bid when the algorithm predicts a high conversion probability (a shopper who just searched for "protein powder" and has purchased whey protein twice in the past 60 days). Brands using Amazon Dynamic Bidding (up and down) reported a 15–20% improvement in total return on ad spend versus manual bidding, while maintaining comparable cost-per-acquisition (Amazon Ads product announcement, 2023).
- **Business Implication**: A brand media director using AI dynamic bidding should set an absolute bid cap — typically 1.5–2x the base max CPC — to prevent algorithmic overbidding during high-competition periods such as Prime Day or holiday peak, when multiple brands' dynamic bids simultaneously inflate category CPCs; the efficiency gains from AI optimisation are fully negated if bid ceilings are absent and cost-per-click spikes to 3–4x during competitive surges.

**Measurement Unification and Unified Commerce Measurement (UCM)**
- **Definition**: An emerging measurement paradigm in which all retailer, media, and brand data — across on-site, off-site, in-store, and CTV — is unified into a single marketing mix model or attribution framework, providing a true total-funnel view of media investment impact.
- **Mechanics**: UCM requires: (1) retailer transaction data (closed-loop sales); (2) retailer media impression data; (3) brand non-retail media exposure data (TV, social, search); and (4) a shared identity layer connecting all three. Data clean rooms provide the privacy-safe infrastructure for UCM. Nielsen and Circana (formerly IRI) are developing UCM frameworks that integrate retail media closed-loop data with their traditional marketing mix modelling infrastructure, allowing brands to compare retail media investment alongside TV, out-of-home, and digital in a single model.
- **Example**: P&G partnered with Circana and Walmart in 2024 to deploy a unified commerce measurement model covering Walmart retail media (on-site and in-store), Google CTV, and Meta social — all measured against Walmart in-store sales as the attribution endpoint. P&G reported that this unified view revealed that Walmart in-store media had been under-credited in prior siloed models: its halo effect on organic category purchases was 2.3x the directly attributed ROAS, changing P&G's internal media mix recommendation for Walmart-centric campaigns significantly (P&G Investor Presentation, 2024).
- **Business Implication**: A CPG brand's marketing analytics team should invest in a UCM infrastructure before allocating retail media budgets above $50M annually — because without a unified view, the standard outcome is that each channel (retail media, TV, social, search) claims full credit for the same purchases, producing inflated ROAS figures across the board that lead finance teams to over-invest in every channel simultaneously rather than making the trade-off decisions that maximise total incremental sales per marketing dollar.

> **Key Takeaway**: The future of retail media is convergence — convergence of on-site, off-site, in-store, and CTV into unified networks; convergence of trade and media budgets into single commerce investment pools; and convergence of disparate measurement standards into common industry frameworks. Retailers and brands that invest in the data infrastructure and organisational alignment to enable this convergence will disproportionately capture the value of the $107B+ retail media market by 2027.

## 13. Practical Application — How Leading Retailers Operationalise Retail Media

### 13.1 Building the Internal RMN Organisation

A retail media network requires four internal capabilities that most retailers must build from scratch. First, a **media platform and bidding team** to manage the ad serving platform (proprietary or licensed), real-time bidding infrastructure, and programmatic platform connectivity. Second, a **data and audience team** to segment loyalty data, manage identity resolution, and govern data clean room partnerships with brands and agencies. Third, a **media sales team** structured analogously to a media company's ad sales organisation — with account executives managing CPG brand relationships, a planning team building custom media packages, and a creative services function supporting brand ad production. Fourth, a **measurement and analytics team** to deliver closed-loop attribution reporting, incrementality studies, and brand lift research.

### 13.2 Retailer Self-Build vs. Partner Platform Decision

Retailers face a fundamental build-vs-buy decision when launching an RMN. Retailers with strong technology capabilities and sufficient scale (typically >$20B in annual revenue and >10M loyalty households) build proprietary platforms — Amazon, Walmart, and Target have each invested $500M+ in proprietary ad infrastructure. Mid-market retailers (annual revenue $2–20B, 2–15M loyalty households) typically license third-party platforms (CitrusAd, Criteo, Epsilon) to launch within 6–12 months at a fraction of the build cost, sharing a percentage of media revenue (typically 15–25%) with the platform vendor.

### 13.3 CPG Brand Organisational Alignment

For CPG brands, the operationalisation challenge is organisational rather than technical. The critical decisions are: (1) who owns the retail media P&L — trade marketing, brand marketing, or a new commerce media team; (2) what the minimum ROAS threshold is for continued investment vs. reallocation — typically 3–4x for on-site and 2.5–3.5x for off-site; and (3) how to prevent internal auction self-competition when multiple brand teams bid on the same keywords at the same retailer. Companies including Unilever, Nestlé, and Reckitt have each created centralised "Commerce Media Centers of Excellence" to govern retail media investment across brands and retailers with consistent measurement standards.

## 14. Key Metrics & Benchmarks

### Core Retail Media Formulas

```
ROAS = Revenue Attributed to Campaign / Total Ad Spend
     Example: $340,000 attributed revenue / $80,000 spend = 4.25x ROAS

iROAS = Incremental Revenue (Treatment − Control) / Ad Spend
      Example: ($340,000 treatment − $210,000 control) / $80,000 = 1.63x iROAS

CPM Cost = (Impressions / 1,000) × CPM Rate
         Example: 5,000,000 impressions × $25 CPM = $125,000 total cost

CPC Total Spend = Clicks × Average CPC
               Example: 12,500 clicks × $0.64 avg CPC = $8,000 total spend

Conversion Rate = Purchases / Clicks
               Example: 1,875 purchases / 12,500 clicks = 15.0% CVR

New-to-Brand Rate = New Brand Buyers / Total Campaign Buyers
                  Example: 750 new buyers / 1,875 total buyers = 40.0% NTB%

Media Revenue Margin = (Media Revenue − Media Operating Costs) / Media Revenue
                     Example: ($1.1B revenue − $0.35B costs) / $1.1B = 68.2% margin
```

| Metric | Formula | Industry Benchmark | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Return on Ad Spend (ROAS)** | `Revenue Attributed / Ad Spend` | 3–6x on-site sponsored; 2–4x off-site | Varies by attribution window; use 7-day last-click for comparability |
| **Incremental ROAS (iROAS)** | `Incremental Revenue / Ad Spend` | 40–65% lower than raw ROAS (GroupM, 2024) | True measure of advertising causality; requires holdout testing |
| **Cost Per Click (CPC)** | `Total Spend / Total Clicks` | $0.40–$5.00 (Amazon); $0.30–$1.50 (Instacart) | Category and keyword competitiveness drive variance |
| **Click-Through Rate (CTR)** | `Clicks / Impressions` | 0.5–1.5% sponsored products; 0.05–0.3% display | Benchmark varies significantly by format and placement |
| **Conversion Rate (CVR)** | `Purchases / Clicks` | 10–20% sponsored products; 5–10% sponsored brands | Higher on retailer sites than standard e-commerce (purchase intent) |
| **Cost Per Acquisition (CPA)** | `Ad Spend / New Customers Acquired` | $8–35 grocery; $15–60 general merchandise | Varies by category and new-vs-existing customer mix |
| **New-to-Brand Rate (NTB%)** | `New Brand Buyers / Total Buyers` | 20–40% target for competitive campaigns | Key efficiency metric for growth-stage brands |
| **Media Revenue as % of Net Sales** | `Media Revenue / Net Sales` | 0.5–1.5% grocery; 3–5% Amazon | Higher = stronger data asset and scale |
| **Share of Voice (SOV)** | `Brand Sponsored Impressions / Total Category Impressions` | 15–25% SOV target for category leader | Direct correlation to organic rank improvement |
| **Ad Fill Rate** | `Impressions Served / Impressions Available` | 60–80% for mature RMNs | Low fill rate = revenue leakage from unsold inventory |

## 15. Case Studies

### Case Study 1: Amazon — Building the World's Largest Retail Media Business

**Retailer**: Amazon.com, Inc. — US-headquartered global marketplace operator and public cloud provider, operating in 20+ countries.
**Challenge**: Amazon entered advertising in the early 2010s with a basic sponsored listing product that was underpriced and under-invested, even as the company possessed arguably the world's most valuable advertising asset: explicit product search intent combined with complete purchase history.
**Approach**: Amazon systematically built a full-stack advertising platform across three phases. Phase 1 (2012–2017): launched Sponsored Products with self-serve auction bidding, growing to $2B+ in revenue. Phase 2 (2018–2022): launched Amazon DSP for programmatic off-site reach, introduced Sponsored Brands and Sponsored Display, acquired IMDb TV (now Freevee), and integrated advertising into Twitch and Amazon Music. Phase 3 (2023–2025): introduced Prime Video advertising (January 2024), expanded International advertising in EU and Japan, launched Amazon Marketing Cloud (AMC) clean room for brand measurement, and deployed AI bidding optimisation across all sponsored formats.
**Outcome**: Amazon Advertising revenue grew from $9.5B in FY2018 to $56.2B in FY2024 — a 491% increase in six years. At an estimated 50% operating margin, Amazon Advertising contributed approximately $28B in operating profit in FY2024, making it more profitable than AWS on an absolute dollar basis in the advertising segment alone. Amazon's share of US digital advertising spend reached approximately 14% in 2024, trailing only Google (28%) and Meta (22%) (eMarketer, 2025).
**Lesson for the industry**: The compounding effect of combining search intent data, purchase history, and owned media inventory across digital and streaming creates an advertising flywheel that becomes more valuable — not less — as the underlying retail business scales; the advertising margin subsidises Prime membership, content investment, and logistics infrastructure, creating a self-reinforcing competitive moat.

### Case Study 2: Kroger Precision Marketing — Monetising Grocery Data at Scale

**Retailer**: The Kroger Co. — largest US supermarket operator by revenue ($150B+ annual sales), operating 2,700+ stores across 35 states under 28 banner names including Kroger, King Soopers, and Harris Teeter.
**Challenge**: Kroger faced structurally declining grocery margins (net margin ~2%) in the face of Amazon/Walmart competition, with over 60 million loyalty households representing an undermonetised first-party data asset — the most detailed grocery purchase database in the US, spanning pharmacy, grocery, fuel, and financial services transactions.
**Approach**: Kroger launched Kroger Precision Marketing (KPM) in 2017 in partnership with 84.51° (Kroger's data analytics subsidiary, a joint venture with Dunnhumby's US operations). KPM structured its offering into three tiers: (1) on-site sponsored products and display on Kroger.com and its 28 banner sites; (2) off-site programmatic advertising using Kroger purchase segments activated via The Trade Desk DSP; and (3) in-store digital screens at checkout and deli/pharmacy counters. KPM built a proprietary clean room integration enabling brands to conduct closed-loop measurement directly within the Kroger transaction dataset without data leaving Kroger's environment.
**Outcome**: KPM reached approximately $1.1B in media revenue in FY2024, with over 1,200 active brand advertisers. At an estimated 68% operating margin, KPM contributed $748M in operating profit — equivalent to Kroger's entire net income from retail operations. KPM's off-site programmatic business, which licenses Kroger purchase segments to brands running campaigns on non-Kroger properties, grew 40% year-over-year in 2023 as brands shifted spend from cookie-dependent targeting to first-party data alternatives (Kroger Annual Report, 2024).
**Lesson for the industry**: A grocery retailer's loyalty programme, historically valued primarily for shopper insights and promotional personalisation, is structurally a high-margin advertising data asset — and the media revenue it generates can match or exceed the operating profit of the core retail business at scale.

### Case Study 3: Target Roundel — Premium Audience, Premium CPMs

**Retailer**: Target Corporation — US mass-market retailer, 1,900+ stores, $109B annual sales (FY2024), demographic skew toward affluent suburban households (median Target shopper HHI ~$80,000 vs. Walmart ~$55,000).
**Challenge**: Target needed to differentiate its retail media offering against Amazon's scale and Walmart's reach by monetising the premium value of its shopper demographic — a high-income, brand-loyal audience that advertisers pay premiums for in traditional media but could not previously access with purchase-level targeting precision.
**Approach**: Target launched Roundel (originally Target Media Network) in 2019, structured as an in-house media company with a proprietary data platform and direct sales team. Roundel's differentiation strategy focused on three dimensions: (1) premium audience CPMs ($30–60) reflecting the Target demographic's higher brand value to advertisers; (2) cross-channel activation spanning Target.com, Target app, in-store digital screens, programmatic display, paid social, and CTV; and (3) creative services capability (RoundElect) enabling brands to produce on-brand ad creative using Target's first-party style guidelines. Roundel partnered with Pinterest in 2021 to allow Target purchase data to be activated directly within Pinterest advertising campaigns, creating an off-site social commerce product unavailable through any other retailer.
**Outcome**: Target Roundel reached approximately $1.5B in media revenue in FY2024, growing from $1B in FY2022. Roundel commands CPMs 40–60% above industry benchmarks for comparable purchase-intent targeting, justified by the Target audience's higher lifetime value to CPG brands. Target's RedCard loyalty members (9M+ active holders) represent a premium within the premium — their purchase frequency, basket size, and brand affinity generate click-to-purchase conversion rates of 18–22% on sponsored product placements, versus 10–14% for standard Target.com visitors (Target Investor Day, 2024).
**Lesson for the industry**: Audience quality multiplies CPM rates in retail media — a retailer with a smaller but wealthier, more brand-loyal shopper base can generate higher media revenue per loyalty member than a higher-volume retailer serving a more price-sensitive demographic, because advertisers pay for conversion probability, not raw reach.

## 16. Glossary

*All key terms from this document, alphabetically sorted. Each definition is standalone — a reader should not need to read the full document to understand a glossary entry.*

| Term | Definition | Also Known As |
|---|---|---|
| **Ad Fill Rate** | The percentage of available ad inventory that is successfully matched to a paying advertiser and served during a given period. | Inventory utilisation rate |
| **Attribution Window** | The defined time period after an ad exposure event (click or impression) during which a subsequent purchase is credited to that ad. | Lookback window, conversion window |
| **Closed-Loop Measurement** | An attribution methodology that connects a specific ad exposure directly to a verified purchase transaction using the retailer's own transaction data, without relying on third-party proxies. | Purchase-based attribution, sales-linked measurement |
| **Connected TV (CTV)** | Internet-connected television devices and streaming services (Hulu, Peacock, Prime Video, Roku) that deliver addressable advertising to households at TV scale with digital targeting precision. | Streaming TV, addressable TV |
| **Cost Per Acquisition (CPA)** | A pricing model in which an advertiser pays only when a defined conversion action — typically a product purchase — is completed. | Pay-per-acquisition, performance-based pricing |
| **Cost Per Click (CPC)** | A pricing model in which an advertiser pays a set fee each time a shopper clicks on an ad unit, used as the primary pricing model for sponsored product placements in retail media. | Pay-per-click, PPC |
| **Cost Per Mille (CPM)** | The cost paid by an advertiser per 1,000 ad impressions delivered, used for display, video, and in-store screen advertising. | Cost per thousand, CPT |
| **Data Clean Room** | A secure computational environment in which two parties (typically a retailer and a brand) can jointly analyse their combined datasets without either party accessing the other's raw individual-level data. | Privacy-preserving collaboration environment |
| **Demand-Side Platform (DSP)** | A technology platform that enables advertisers to programmatically purchase digital advertising inventory across multiple publishers and ad exchanges through automated, auction-based buying. | Programmatic buying platform |
| **Endemic Advertiser** | A brand that sells its products through a retailer and purchases advertising within that retailer's media network to drive visibility and conversion for those listed products. | In-category advertiser, supplier advertiser |
| **First-Party Data** | Data collected directly by a retailer from its own customers through transactions, loyalty programme enrolment, website behaviour, and app usage — owned entirely by the retailer and not sourced from third parties, making it the foundational asset of any retail media network. | 1P data, owned data |
| **Incremental ROAS (iROAS)** | The return on ad spend calculated using only the revenue generated above the organic baseline that would have occurred without advertising, measured through controlled holdout experiments. | Incrementality-adjusted ROAS, lift-based ROAS |
| **In-Store Retail Media** | Advertising placements within a physical retail store environment — including digital screens, audio, shelf-edge displays, and smart cart screens — that reach shoppers at the point of purchase. | Point-of-purchase media, physical retail media |
| **Kroger Precision Marketing (KPM)** | Kroger's retail media network and data services business, powered by 60M+ loyalty household transaction data, offering on-site, off-site programmatic, and closed-loop measurement products. | — |
| **Marketing Mix Model (MMM)** | A statistical modelling approach that quantifies the revenue contribution of each marketing and media investment — including retail media, TV, digital, and promotion — using historical sales and spend data to optimise future budget allocation. | Media mix model, econometric model |
| **Match Rate** | The percentage of a retailer's hashed loyalty member records that successfully match to user profiles on an external advertising platform (e.g., Meta, Google), determining the proportion of the loyalty audience that can be targeted with off-site campaigns. | Identity match rate, audience match rate |
| **New-to-Brand Rate (NTB%)** | The percentage of purchases generated by an advertising campaign that come from shoppers who have not purchased that brand in the prior 12 months, measuring the campaign's new customer acquisition efficiency. | New buyer rate, new customer acquisition rate |
| **Non-Endemic Advertiser** | A brand that does not sell products through the retailer but purchases advertising within the retailer's media network to reach the retailer's shopper audience — typically from categories such as financial services, automotive, travel, insurance, or telecommunications. | Out-of-category advertiser, non-supplier advertiser |
| **Off-Site Retail Media** | The use of a retailer's first-party shopper data to deliver targeted advertising on external digital platforms — programmatic display, social media, CTV, and search — outside the retailer's own properties. | Extended audience, data syndication |
| **On-Site Retail Media** | Paid advertising placements within a retailer's owned digital properties — website, mobile app, and email — including sponsored products, sponsored brands, and display formats. | Retailer.com advertising, on-site sponsored |
| **Quality Score** | A composite metric used by retail media ad auction engines to rank competing advertisers, calculated from the predicted click-through rate of an ad and its relevance to the shopper's search query — higher quality scores win auctions at lower bid prices than low-quality-score competitors bidding the same amount. | Relevance score, ad rank multiplier |
| **Retail Media Network (RMN)** | A formal advertising business operated by a retailer that packages its owned media inventory, first-party shopper data, and closed-loop measurement capability into a structured advertising offering sold to brand advertisers. | Commerce media network, retail advertising platform |
| **Retail Media Platform (RMP)** | The technology infrastructure that powers a retailer's advertising business — including inventory management, auction-based bidding, ad serving, reporting, and billing — deployed either as a proprietary build or a licensed SaaS solution. | Retail ad server, commerce media platform |
| **Return on Ad Spend (ROAS)** | The ratio of revenue attributed to an advertising campaign to the total advertising spend of that campaign, used as the primary efficiency metric in retail media. | Ad revenue efficiency ratio |
| **Second-Price Auction** | An ad auction mechanism in which the winning bidder pays the second-highest bid amount plus one cent, incentivising advertisers to bid their true maximum willingness to pay. | Vickrey auction |
| **Shelf-Edge Display** | A digital or electronic display unit mounted at the product shelf level that delivers dynamic branded content, promotional messaging, or video advertising to shoppers at the exact moment of product selection — the closest physical advertising proximity to the point of purchase available in a retail store. | Electronic shelf label advertising, ESL media, shelf-edge screen |
| **Share of Voice (SOV)** | The percentage of total advertising impressions or sponsored placements within a product category that a specific brand's campaigns capture, used to measure competitive presence in retail media. | Category SOV, search SOV |
| **Smart Cart** | A shopping cart equipped with a digital screen, item scanning capability, and weight-based purchase verification that enables in-session ad serving and closed-loop attribution as shoppers navigate the store. | Connected cart, Caper Cart |
| **Sponsored Brands** | A retail media ad format that displays a brand's logo, custom headline, and multiple products in a banner placement at the top of search results, designed for brand awareness combined with multi-product discovery. | Headline search ads |
| **Sponsored Products** | A keyword-triggered retail media ad format that serves a specific product listing in premium positions within retailer search results or category browse pages, priced on a cost-per-click basis. | Product listing ads, keyword-targeted ads |
| **Unified Commerce Measurement (UCM)** | An emerging measurement approach that consolidates all media exposure data — on-site, off-site, in-store, and CTV — with retailer transaction data into a single attribution or marketing mix model, enabling true total-funnel evaluation of retail media investment. | Total commerce measurement, cross-channel attribution |
| **Walmart Connect** | Walmart's retail media network offering sponsored products, display, in-store screens, and programmatic advertising using Walmart's 140M weekly shopper transaction data. | Walmart Media Group (former name) |
